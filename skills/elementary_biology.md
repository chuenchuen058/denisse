# Elementary biology

When asked a basic biology question (living things, plants, animals, the
human body, ecosystems):

1. Keep it concrete -- use familiar plants/animals/body parts as examples
   before any abstract term.
2. Ground answers in these core facts:
   - Living things grow, need energy (food), reproduce, respond to their
     surroundings, and are made of cells (the basic unit of life).
     Non-living things (rocks, toys) don't do these things.
   - Plants make their own food from sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide
     (photosynthesis), releasing oxygen -- this is why plants sit at the
     base of most food chains.
   - Animals get energy by eating plants and/or other animals
     (herbivore/carnivore/omnivore); a food chain shows who eats whom, and
     a food web is many food chains linked together.
   - Life cycles: most living things follow a repeating pattern (e.g. seed
     -> sprout -> plant -> flower -> seed; egg -> caterpillar -> chrysalis
     -> butterfly) -- not every animal's cycle looks like a human's.
   - The human body has systems that work together: skeletal (bones,
     support), muscular (movement), circulatory (heart and blood, carries
     oxygen/nutrients), respiratory (lungs, gas exchange), digestive
     (breaks down food for energy), nervous (brain/nerves, control and
     senses).
   - The five senses (sight, hearing, smell, taste, touch) let the body
     detect its surroundings through specific organs (eyes, ears, nose,
     tongue, skin).
   - An ecosystem is all the living things and their environment
     interacting together (a pond, a forest); a habitat is the specific
     place a living thing is adapted to live in.
   - Adaptation: a feature that helps a living thing survive in its
     environment (a polar bear's fur for cold, a cactus's spines to reduce
     water loss) -- built up over generations, not chosen by the
     individual.
3. Correct a misconception directly if the question implies one (e.g.
   "plants aren't alive because they don't move" -- no, they meet all the
   criteria for life, they just don't move the way animals do).
4. Keep any body/health-related answer age-appropriate and factual, not
   medical advice.
5. If unsure of a specific fact, say so rather than guessing.
