# Elementary chemistry

When asked a basic chemistry question (atoms, elements, mixtures, states of
matter, simple reactions):

1. Keep it concrete -- use kitchen/everyday examples (salt dissolving in
   water, baking soda and vinegar fizzing, ice melting) before any abstract
   term.
2. Ground answers in these core facts:
   - Everything is made of atoms -- tiny particles too small to see. An
     element is a substance made of only one type of atom (e.g. oxygen,
     gold, iron); the periodic table lists all known elements.
   - A molecule/compound forms when atoms of different elements bond
     together (e.g. water = H2O = 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom).
   - A mixture is two or more substances physically combined but not
     chemically bonded, and can usually be separated back out (e.g. salad,
     sand and water). A solution is a mixture where one substance dissolves
     evenly into another (e.g. salt in water).
   - States of matter (solid/liquid/gas) are about how tightly packed and
     how fast the particles move, not what they're made of -- the same
     substance can be any of the three (ice/water/steam are all H2O).
   - A chemical reaction makes a new substance with different properties
     (rust forming, wood burning, dough baking); a physical change
     (melting, cutting, dissolving) doesn't -- it's still fundamentally the
     same stuff.
   - Acids (sour, e.g. lemon juice, vinegar) and bases (bitter/slippery,
     e.g. soap, baking soda) sit at opposite ends of the pH scale; mixing
     them can neutralize both.
   - Burning/combustion needs fuel, oxygen, and heat -- remove any one and
     it stops.
3. Correct a misconception directly if the question implies one (e.g.
   "mixing paint colors is a chemical reaction" -- no, that's a physical
   mixture).
4. Never describe an actual dangerous experiment (mixing real household
   chemicals, real fire) as something to go try -- explain the concept,
   don't give a how-to for a hazard.
5. If unsure of a specific fact (an exact formula, a melting point), say so
   rather than guessing.
